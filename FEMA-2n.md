# FEMA 1999, Section 2 — the currency definitions

*In this Act, unless the context otherwise requires,—*

**(h)** *"currency" includes all currency notes, postal notes, postal orders, money orders, cheques, drafts, travellers cheques, letters of credit, bills of exchange and promissory notes, credit cards or such other similar instruments, as may be notified by the Reserve Bank;*

**(m)** *"foreign currency" means any currency other than Indian currency;*

**(n)** *"foreign exchange" means foreign currency and includes,—*
*(i) deposits, credits and balances payable in any foreign currency,*
*(ii) drafts, travellers cheques, letters of credit or bills of exchange, expressed or drawn in Indian currency but payable in any foreign currency,*
*(iii) drafts, travellers cheques, letters of credit or bills of exchange drawn by banks, institutions or persons outside India, but payable in Indian currency;*

**(q)** *"Indian currency" means currency which is expressed or drawn in Indian rupees but does not include special bank notes and special one rupee notes issued under section 28A of the Reserve Bank of India Act, 1934;*

# ⭐⭐ THIS IS NO LONGER AN INFERENCE

We have been saying, carefully, that the FEMA position is *"an inference from s.2(n) read with ss.7–8, not an explicit prohibition."*

**Half of that is now wrong, and in our favour.**

## The chain within FEMA

| Step | Text |
|---|---|
| 1 | **(h)** "currency" is a **closed list** of instruments — notes, orders, cheques, drafts, travellers cheques, letters of credit, bills of exchange, promissory notes, credit cards — *plus* "such other similar instruments **as may be notified by the Reserve Bank**" |
| 2 | A stablecoin is **not in the list**, and **the RBI has notified no such instrument** |
| 3 | **(m)** "foreign currency" = any currency other than Indian currency. Not currency → not foreign currency |
| 4 | **(n)** "foreign exchange" = foreign currency, plus deposits/drafts/cheques *payable in* foreign currency. A stablecoin is none of these |

**So USDC is not foreign exchange under FEMA. That follows from the text, not from commentary.**

## ⭐ AND THE INCOME-TAX ACT SAYS SO EXPRESSLY

This is the part that changes the claim.

**Section 2(47A) of the Income-tax Act defines "virtual digital asset" as:**

> *"any information or code or number or token **(not being Indian currency or foreign currency)**, generated through cryptographic means or otherwise…"*

**And its Explanation (b) states:**

> *"the expressions 'currency', 'foreign currency' and 'Indian currency' shall have the same meanings as respectively assigned to them in **clauses (h), (m) and (q) of section 2 of the Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999**."*

### Read that again

**The Income-tax Act defines a virtual digital asset as, by definition, NOT foreign currency — and it reaches across into FEMA to borrow the meaning.**

The two Acts are not merely consistent. **Parliament wrote the exclusion into the definition of the asset itself, and pointed at FEMA to do it.**

## What we may now say, and what remains inference

| Claim | Status |
|---|---|
| A stablecoin is not foreign currency or foreign exchange | ✅ **Statutory. Expressly, in two Acts, by cross-reference** |
| Export proceeds must be realised in foreign exchange through authorised channels | ✅ Statutory — FEMA ss.7–8 |
| **Therefore receiving USDC for exported services is non-compliant** | ⚠️ **Still an inference** — but now an inference from two express provisions rather than one |

**Update the language everywhere:** not *"crypto is arguably not foreign exchange"* but *"the Income-tax Act defines a virtual digital asset as one that is not foreign currency, expressly adopting FEMA's definition. The only inference left is the consequence, not the characterisation."*
