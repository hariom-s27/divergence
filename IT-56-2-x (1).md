# Section 56(2)(x) — receipts without or for inadequate consideration

**(x)** *where any person receives, in any previous year, from any person or persons on or after the 1st day of April, 2017,—*
*…*
*(c) any property, other than immovable property,—*
*(A) without consideration, the aggregate fair market value of which exceeds fifty thousand rupees, the whole of the aggregate fair market value of such property;*
*(B) for a consideration which is less than the aggregate fair market value of the property by an amount exceeding fifty thousand rupees, the aggregate fair market value of such property as exceeds such consideration…*

## ⭐⭐⭐ THE CURRENT EXPLANATION TO CLAUSE (x) — VERBATIM

*Explanation.—For the purposes of this clause,—*

***(a)*** *the expressions "assessable", **"fair market value"**, "jewellery", "relative" and "stamp duty value" shall have the same meanings as respectively assigned to them in the Explanation to clause (vii); and*

***(b)*** *the expression **"property"** shall have the same meaning as assigned to it in clause (d) of the Explanation to clause (vii) **and shall include virtual digital asset**;*

## The Explanation to clause (vii) — the definitions that matter

*Explanation.—For the purposes of this clause,—*

**(b)** ***"fair market value" of a property, other than an immovable property, means the value determined in accordance with the method as may be prescribed;***

**(d)** *"property" means the following **capital asset** of the assessee, namely:—*
*(i) immovable property being land or building or both; (ii) shares and securities; (iii) jewellery; (iv) archaeological collections; (v) drawings; (vi) paintings; (vii) sculptures; (viii) any work of art; or (ix) bullion;*

---

# ⭐⭐⭐ LIMB (b) IS THE SENTENCE THE WHOLE THESIS NEEDED

> ***"fair market value" … MEANS the value determined in accordance with the method as may be prescribed***

Read the verb. **Means.** Not *"may be determined by"*, not *"shall ordinarily be"*, not *"is the open market price"*.

**The Act does not define fair market value as a real-world quantity. It defines it as whatever the prescribed method produces.**

> ## **If no method is prescribed, there is no fair market value in law. Not an uncertain one — none.**

**That is the strongest possible form of our claim, and it is one sentence of primary statute.**

Compare **s.2(22B)**, the Act's general definition, which does at least give a fallback: *"the price that the capital asset would ordinarily fetch on sale in the open market."* **Section 56 has no such fallback.** It goes straight to the prescribed method and stops.

## The closed list — and a second gap

Limb (d) defines "property" as **a closed list of nine capital assets.** Land, shares, jewellery, archaeological collections, drawings, paintings, sculptures, works of art, bullion.

**And note it says "the following capital asset."** So s.56 property is a capital-asset concept. Our freelancer's receipt is business income under s.28 — outside this head entirely.

## ⭐⭐⭐ THE OMISSION IS VISIBLE INSIDE A SINGLE AMENDED SENTENCE

**Look at what the drafter did, in one Explanation, in one amendment.**

| Limb | What it does | What it says about VDAs |
|---|---|---|
| **(a)** | Imports **"fair market value"** from clause (vii) — which says FMV **means the value determined by the prescribed method** | **Untouched** |
| **(b)** | Imports **"property"** from clause (vii)(d) — **"and shall include virtual digital asset"** | **Expressly extended** |

> ## **In the same Explanation, in the same amendment, Parliament extended "property" to cover virtual digital assets — and left "fair market value" pointing at a prescribing rule that does not reach them.**

**This is no longer an argument about what the law fails to mention.** The two limbs sit beside each other. One was edited to include VDAs. The other was not, and it is the one that says how to value them.

**The gap is inside a single sentence that Parliament amended, and it is visible on the face of it.**

## Why this is the strongest form of the claim

We began by saying *"no rule covers this."* That is a claim about absence, and absence is hard to prove.

**We can now say something narrower and much harder to answer:**

> *Parliament brought virtual digital assets into section 56 by name. Section 56 says their value is whatever the prescribed method says. Four years later, no method has been prescribed. The Act asks a question of itself and does not answer.*

**Every clause of that sentence is primary text held in this corpus.**
