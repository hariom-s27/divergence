## Q2 / U11 — The 2025 Act equivalent of Section 270A ⭐ RESOLVED

**This was flagged as the highest priority open item, because the entire value proposition rests on it.**

**Answer: Section 270A → Section 439, Income-tax Act, 2025, effective 1 April 2026.**

<cite index="88-1">Section 270A of the Income Tax Act 1961 is now replaced with Section 439 of the Income Tax Act 2025.</cite> <cite index="90-1">The replacement takes effect from 01.04.2026.</cite>

And the immunity route moved too. <cite index="89-1">Section 270AA allows an assessee to seek immunity from penalty and prosecution, applying in Form 68 within one month of the order. The 2025 Act carries the same idea forward: Section 440 provides immunity from penalty under Section 439, with Form 161 replacing Form 68.</cite>

**Critically, the sub-section numbering changed as well — and our value proposition cites a sub-section.**

<cite index="91-1">Section 439(8) provides the exceptions: bona fide explanations with full disclosures, income determined by estimation with correct books, self-estimated lower additions disclosed properly, and additions matching arm's length price with full documentation. Penalty under 439(9) is 50% of tax payable on under-reported income; 439(10) raises it to 200% for misreporting; 439(11) defines misreporting as misrepresentation, suppression of facts, unrecorded investments or receipts, false entries, unsubstantiated expenses, and failure to report international transactions.</cite>

The two limbs we rely on, verbatim: <cite index="91-1">439(8)(a) — the amount of income in respect of which the assessee offers an explanation and the Competent Authority is satisfied that the explanation is bona fide and the assessee has disclosed all the material facts to substantiate it; 439(8)(b) — the amount of under-reported income determined on the basis of an estimate, if the accounts are correct and complete to the satisfaction of the Competent Authority.</cite>

### The mapping we now use

| Old | New | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| s.270A | **s.439** | Penalty for under-reporting / misreporting |
| **s.270A(6)** | **s.439(8)** | **The exclusions — our value proposition** |
| s.270A(6)(a) | **s.439(8)(a)** | Bona fide explanation + all material facts |
| s.270A(6)(b) | **s.439(8)(b)** | Estimate where the basis was disclosed |
| s.270A(7) | s.439(9) | 50% |
| s.270A(8) | s.439(10) | 200% |
| s.270A(9) | s.439(11) | Misreporting categories |
| s.270AA | s.440 | Immunity |
| Form 68 | **Form 161** | Immunity application |

**⚠️ This means every mention of "270A(6)" in our material is now a stale citation** — exactly the error we caught in Step 7, made a second time, on a different provision. We caught it ourselves this time, which is progress of a sort.

### Note on 439(8)(b)

There is a condition we had not registered: the estimate exclusion requires that **"the accounts are correct and complete."** So the exclusion is not automatic — it depends on the rest of the books being sound. Our output supports the *disclosure of the basis*; it does not fix bad bookkeeping. **Say this openly. It is a limitation, and stating it is a scoring point.**

## U16 / U17 — Does anyone quantify and disclose uncertain tax positions? ⭐ RESOLVED, AND IT IS THE BIGGEST PRIOR ART YET

**Yes. It is a mature enterprise practice with its own accounting standard and dedicated software, and we had completely missed it.**

**Thomson Reuters ONESOURCE Uncertain Tax Positions** (formerly TaxStream FIN 48). <cite index="93-1">It helps analyse and report the tax treatment of open positions under ASC 740-10-50 and IAS 37, keeping an organised inventory of all positions, calculating tax and interest for each one, and reporting on them with a full audit trail — while giving the information needed to decide what to disclose.</cite>

Read that again. *Inventory of uncertain positions · tax calculated per position · full audit trail · decide what to disclose.* That is structurally our product, shipped, for corporations, since roughly 2007.

**Behind it sits ASC 740-10-50 (formerly FIN 48)** — a US accounting standard requiring companies to identify uncertain tax positions, assess whether each is more-likely-than-not to be sustained, measure it, and disclose it. **The concept of a formally managed, quantified, disclosed uncertain tax position is not new. It is an accounting standard.**

### Why this is a gift, not a wound

We are not the first to think tax uncertainty should be quantified and disclosed. **We are the first to bring it down to where it is missing.**

| | FIN 48 / ONESOURCE | Us |
|---|---|---|
| Who | Corporations | Individuals and their CAs |
| Reporting to | Investors, via financial statements | The assessing officer, via the return |
| Granularity | Position level, entered by a tax director | **Transaction level, detected automatically** |
| Detection | **A human decides it is uncertain** | **The system finds it** |
| Jurisdiction | US GAAP / IFRS | **No Indian individual equivalent exists** |
| Trigger | Annual reporting cycle | At the moment of the transaction |

**The reframe, and it is much stronger than what we had:**

> Large corporations have had a formal framework for uncertain tax positions since 2006 — a standard, an inventory, a reserve, a disclosure. A freelancer in Pune has a CoinGecko screenshot. We are bringing an established professional discipline down to the transaction level, for the people who never got it.

**That is far more credible than claiming to invent a category.** It puts us alongside IFRS 13, the auditor's disclaimer of opinion, and the metrology uncertainty budget — the fourth borrowed pattern, and the closest one yet.

**And it answers the hostile question before it lands.** *"Isn't this just FIN 48?"* → *"Yes, conceptually — and that is the point. FIN 48 exists because uncertain positions need managing. It applies to corporations with tax directors. Nobody built it for the person filing ITR-3."*

### Also found, and worth naming
- **Blue J** — AI tax outcome prediction; the comparison notes it is for *"controversy work where you want to assess position risk before you commit."* Different objective: they predict how a position will fare; we report that no determinate position exists.
- **BayesJudge** (2024) — Bayesian uncertainty in legal judgment prediction. Predicts court outcomes with confidence intervals. Again: predicting an outcome, not reporting statutory silence.

## U8 — Has the bona fide / estimate exclusion actually worked in practice?

**Partial evidence, and it points in a useful direction.**

A tribunal decision found in this pass: <cite index="92-1">the Assessing Officer made additions under deeming provisions and initiated penalty for "misreporting." The Tribunal held that where income is computed solely by legislative deeming, the case does not fall within "under-reporting" at all. The penalty notice also failed to specify the precise limb — under-reporting or misreporting — and mere use of the word "misreporting" without particulars was termed arbitrary.</cite>

**Two useful things:**
1. Tribunals **do** scrutinise the under-reporting / misreporting classification and quash penalties where the department has been sloppy. The distinction is live, not theoretical.
2. The specific point — *has 439(8)(b)/270A(6)(b) succeeded on a valuation-method dispute* — is **still not found.**

**Honest position:** the mechanism is real and litigated. Whether it has been won specifically on a valuation-method disagreement is unverified. **Say the mechanism exists; do not claim it is proven for our exact fact pattern.**
