# THE RULE FOR THIS DOCUMENT

**No judging. None.** Not "that won't work," not "we don't have time," not even a raised eyebrow.

This is harder than it sounds, and there is a reason for it. **Evaluating and generating use opposite mental postures.** Judging while generating kills the strange ideas first — and strange ideas are the only ones worth doing this exercise for. The obvious ideas will still be there in an hour. The odd ones vanish the moment you frown at them.

Criticism happens in Step 12. Today we only make things.

# TECHNIQUE 1 — VARY THE AXIS

Hold the root cause fixed — *AI fabricates answers where the world has not decided* — and change one variable at a time.

## Vary the domain

**1. DIVERGENCE-Tax** *(current)*
Indian crypto receipt. Shows both valuations, prices the gap, records the election.

**2. DIVERGENCE-Health**
Flags where clinical guidelines are simply silent — dosing in pregnancy, paediatrics, renal impairment. The guideline doesn't say, so the AI invents a number.

**3. DIVERGENCE-Claims**
Insurance. "Reasonable and customary" has no published schedule. Shows the range and who decided.

**4. DIVERGENCE-Work**
Contractor vs employee. A multi-factor test with no formula, no bright line, and enormous consequences.

**5. DIVERGENCE-Build**
Building codes against materials the code predates. No rule exists yet.

**6. DIVERGENCE-Content**
Platform policy applied to a case the policy never imagined. Shows the moderator that the rule doesn't reach.

## Vary the user

**7. Officer View**
Built for the *assessing officer*, not the taxpayer. One screen: here is the declared basis, here is the alternative, here is what the taxpayer said they knew. Speeds up his work rather than defending against him.

**8. Regulator View**
Aggregate every lacuna the system encounters and hand CBDT a ranked list: *"these are the twelve places your rules don't reach, ordered by how often people hit them."* **The tool that finds gaps becomes the input to closing them.**

**9. Platform-side**
Upwork, Deel, Fiverr embed it at payout. The valuation record is created at the moment the money moves, by the platform, for everyone.

**10. Exchange-side**
CoinDCX or WazirX issues a valuation certificate at withdrawal, stating which rate was used and what the alternative was.

**11. Model-vendor-side**
Sold to OpenAI or Google as a safety layer: *before your model answers a regulated question, check whether the question has an answer.*

## Vary the output

**12. Uncertainty Score**
One portable number, credit-score shaped, attached to any AI answer. *"This answer scores 340/900 on determinacy."* Third parties understand it without understanding the domain.

**13. AI Nutrition Label**
A standardised block appended to any AI output: sources consulted · date last checked · choices made · what was assumed. Like the panel on a food packet — boring, standard, and impossible to ignore once people expect it.

**14. Inline Underline**
Spell-checker shaped. A red squiggle under any sentence in an AI answer that asserts something the law doesn't settle. No report, no dashboard — just a mark where the problem is.

**15. The Refusal Router**
Doesn't answer at all. Detects that a question has no determinate answer and routes it to a human, with the reason attached.

## Vary the timing

**16. Pre-Transaction Advisor**
Runs *before* the payment. *"If you accept USDC for this invoice, here is what you are signing up for: no FIRC, GST zero-rating at risk, and a valuation with no prescribed method."* Prevention rather than documentation.

**17. Post-Notice Remediator**
Dormant until a notice arrives, then reconstructs the most defensible version of the record from whatever exists. Sells at the moment of maximum pain.

# TECHNIQUE 2 — STEAL THE SHAPE

Take a thing that exists in the world and ask what it looks like applied to our root cause.

**18. The Linter** *(shape: ESLint, static analysis)*
Runs over AI output the way a linter runs over code. Rules like `no-definitive-claim-on-undecided-question`, `require-citation-date`, `no-single-valuation-without-alternative`. Warnings and errors, configurable, in your editor. **Developers already know this shape completely.**

**19. Second Opinion** *(shape: medical second opinion)*
You paste any AI tax answer — from ChatGPT, from a competitor, from anywhere — and it tells you what that answer *hid*. Needs no documents, no integration, no parsing.

**20. Smoke Detector** *(shape: smoke alarm)*
Always on, does nothing 99% of the time, and makes a noise when a specific condition appears. A browser extension that watches AI answers as you read them and beeps on a Class 3 claim.

**21. Flight Recorder** *(shape: aircraft black box)*
Records everything at the moment, unread unless there's an incident. Nobody looks at it until something goes wrong — and then it's the only thing that matters.

**22. Devil's Advocate** *(shape: advocatus diaboli — the Vatican role created in 1587 whose entire job was to argue against sainthood)*
A formal, paid, institutional role for arguing against a conclusion. As a service: submit your position, receive the strongest case against it.

**23. The Checklist** *(shape: aviation pre-flight, surgical safety checklist)*
**No AI at all.** A one-page forcing structure a CA fills in for any cross-border receipt. Twelve questions. The value is that it makes you *stop* at the points where people skip under time pressure — which is exactly what Gawande found in surgery.

**24. Git Blame for Legal Positions** *(shape: version control)*
Every figure in a return carries a history: who decided, when, on what basis, and what the alternative was. `git blame` on your tax return.

# TECHNIQUE 3 — EXTREMES

## The one-hour version
**25. The Prompt**
A single, carefully written prompt anyone can paste into ChatGPT that forces the disclosure structure — both valuations, the missing documents, the certainty labels. Given away free. No product, no code.

## The one-day version
**26. The Lacuna List**
A single public web page: **"The 12 places Indian tax law has no answer for crypto."** Each with the provision that fails, the date checked, and what practitioners actually do. No AI, no login, no build.

## The one-year version
**27. The Standard**
An open specification for uncertainty disclosure in AI outputs — a schema, a registry of known lacunae, a badge. Not a product. **Infrastructure that other people build on.**

# TECHNIQUE 4 — MINE: INVERT THE BENEFICIARY

Ask who *loses* if this works, and then check whether you had the customer backwards.

**28. Sell to the Department**
We assumed the tax department is the adversary. It isn't. **An officer wants a legible basis as much as the taxpayer does** — it saves him two letters and a dispute. A disclosure format the department itself endorses would be adopted by everyone overnight.

# TECHNIQUE 5 — MINE: REMOVE THE CENTRAL COMPONENT

Delete the thing you assume is essential and see what remains.

**Delete the AI** → idea 23, the checklist.
**Delete the documents** → idea 19, second opinion on a pasted answer.
**Delete the user interface** → 
**29. The API Header**
A response header on any regulated-domain API call: `X-Determinacy: contested; methods=2; spread=8.5%`. Machines read it. Nobody has to look at anything.

---

# TECHNIQUE 6 — MINE: MAKE IT A DATA PRODUCT

**30. The Divergence Index**
Publish, daily and publicly, how far the two valuation methods are apart. A number, a chart, an RSS feed. **Bloomberg-terminal shaped.** On 28–29 June it would have spiked to 8.5% and anyone watching would have known that day, not eleven months later.

**31. The Lacuna Wiki**
Community-maintained registry of known gaps in Indian tax law, with dates and sources. Wikipedia for what the law doesn't say. **Solves the corpus-rot problem by making it everyone's problem.**

---

# TECHNIQUE 7 — MINE: COMBINE TWO EXISTING IDEAS

**32. Linter + Score** — the linter emits warnings; the score summarises them into one portable number for people who won't read warnings.

**33. Checklist + Second Opinion** — the human fills the checklist, the AI argues with what they filled in.

**34. Index + Pre-Transaction Advisor** — the public divergence index *triggers* the advisor. When the spread crosses 5%, everyone who might be affected gets told.

# FOUR THAT SURPRISED ME

*Noting surprise is not evaluation. These are the ones I did not expect to write.*

**#19 Second Opinion** — because it needs **no document parsing at all.** You paste an answer, it tells you what was hidden. That is a dramatically smaller build than the seven-node pipeline, and the demo is 90 seconds: paste a ChatGPT answer on stage, watch the tool dismantle it.

**#23 The Checklist** — because deleting the AI entirely from an AI-track project is the kind of idea that only appears if you force yourself to remove the component you're most attached to.

**#26 The Lacuna List** — because the one-day version might be more *useful to real people* than the full product, and it's a demo asset whatever else you build.

**#28 Sell to the Department** — because we had assumed an adversary for eight steps without checking. The officer wants a legible basis too.
