# Rule 207, Income-tax Rules, 2026 — Rate of exchange for TDS on income payable in foreign currency

<!-- VERBATIM-START -->
**207. Rate of exchange for purpose of deduction of tax at source on income payable in foreign currency.—** (1) For the purpose of deduction of tax at source on any income payable in foreign currency as specified in sub-rule (2), the rate of exchange for calculation of the value in rupees shall be the telegraphic transfer buying rate of such currency as on the date on which such tax is required to be deducted under the provisions of Chapter XIX-B of the Act; but where the telegraphic transfer buying rate is not published on such date, the last such published rate may be taken.

(2) The provisions of sub-rule (1) shall be applicable in respect of income payable—

(a) to an assessee outside India; or

(b) to a Unit located in an International Financial Services Centre; or

(c) by a Unit located in an International Financial Services Centre, to an assessee in India.

(3) For the purposes of this rule,—

(a) "International Financial Services Centre" shall have the same meaning as assigned to it in section 2(q) of the Special Economic Zones Act, 2005 (28 of 2005);

(b) **"telegraphic transfer buying rate", in relation to a foreign currency, means the rate or rates of exchange adopted by the State Bank of India constituted under the State Bank of India Act, 1955 (23 of 1955), for buying such currency, having regard to the guidelines specified from time to time by the Reserve Bank of India for buying such currency, where such currency is made available to that bank through a telegraphic transfer;** and

(c) "Unit" shall have the meaning assigned to it in section 2(zc) of the Special Economic Zones Act, 2005 (28 of 2005).

# ⭐ WHY THIS FILE MATTERS MORE THAN EXPECTED

## 1. It is the only authority for saying "SBI"

Rule 206 prescribes "the telegraphic transfer buying rate" and defines nothing. **Sub-rule 207(3)(b) is where the State Bank of India appears** — by name, by its constituting Act. Without this file in the corpus, the phrase "SBI TT buying rate" in our own outputs has no citation behind it. It is now cited.

## 2. ⭐⭐ It contains a stale-date fallback — and Rule 206 does not

Read 207(1) carefully:

> *"…but where the telegraphic transfer buying rate is **not published on such date**, the **last such published rate** may be taken."*

**The drafter anticipated the exact problem our case turns on** — that a rate may not be published on the relevant date — and wrote a rule for it.

**But that fallback lives in Rule 207, which governs TDS on income payable to an assessee outside India.** It is not in Rule 206, which governs conversion of income received by a resident.

> **This is the same asymmetry as Rule 57 row 7, in a different rule.**
> **A solution to our exact problem exists in the notified Rules. It is scoped to a different provision.**

| | Deducting TDS on a payment **out** to a non-resident | Converting income **received** by a resident |
|---|---|---|
| Governing rule | Rule 207 | Rule 206 |
| Rate not published on the date? | ✅ *"the last such published rate may be taken"* | ❌ **no fallback provided** |

**Two independent instances of the same drafting pattern is much stronger than one.** One looks like an oversight. Two looks like the shape of the instrument — provisions written for outbound and deemed-income situations, with the inbound receipt left unprovided for.

## 3. And 207 does not reach our case anyway — twice over

- **Sub-rule (2) limits it** to income payable to an assessee outside India, or to/from an IFSC Unit. Our freelancer is a resident receiving income. None of the three limbs applies.
- **"foreign currency"** again. The Act's VDA definition excludes VDAs from being foreign currency, so even the gateway words do not fit.

# ✅ THE Q&A CHAIN, COMPLETE

> **Q. "Why not just use the TT buying rate? Rule 206 says so."**
>
> Rule 206 is the renumbered Rule 115. It opens *"income… in foreign currency"*, and the Act's own VDA definition excludes a VDA from being foreign currency, so it does not reach the receipt.
>
> **And if it did**, its specified date for business income is *"the last day of the tax year"* — 31 March, not the day of receipt.
>
> **And if you then asked what happens when no rate is published on the relevant date** — the Rules do answer that. In **Rule 207(1)**: take the last published rate. But Rule 207 governs TDS on income payable to a person outside India. **The fallback exists, and it is pointed somewhere else.**

**Rehearsed follow-up — "then analogise, apply 207's fallback anyway":**
> Analogy is not prescription. And the two available analogies disagree with each other: Rule 206 would send you to 31 March, Rule 207's fallback would send you to Friday 26 June. **Two official-flavoured answers that disagree is the finding, not a hole in it.**
