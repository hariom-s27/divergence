# Section 115BBH — Tax on income from virtual digital asset
<!-- VERBATIM-START -->

**115BBH. (1)** *Where the total income of an assessee includes any income from the transfer of any virtual digital asset, notwithstanding anything contained in any other provision of this Act, the income-tax payable shall be the aggregate of,—*
*(a) the amount of income-tax calculated on the income from transfer of such virtual digital asset at the rate of thirty per cent; and*
*(b) the amount of income-tax with which the assessee would have been chargeable, had the total income of the assessee been reduced by the income referred to in clause (a).*

**(2)** *Notwithstanding anything contained in any other provision of this Act,—*
*(a) no deduction in respect of any expenditure (other than cost of acquisition, if any) or allowance or set off of any loss shall be allowed to the assessee under any provision of this Act in computing the income referred to in clause (a) of sub-section (1); and*
*(b) no set off of loss from transfer of the virtual digital asset computed under clause (a) of sub-section (1) shall be allowed against income computed under any provision of this Act to the assessee and such loss shall not be allowed to be carried forward to succeeding assessment years.*

**(3)** *For the purposes of this section, the word "transfer" as defined in clause (47) of section 2, shall apply to any virtual digital asset, whether capital asset or not.*

# WHAT THIS PROVES — AND WHAT IT CONSPICUOUSLY DOES NOT

## ✅ It confirms the two-stage model, from the statute

**115BBH taxes "income from the **transfer** of any virtual digital asset."**

Our freelancer **receives** USDC. She does not transfer it. **So 115BBH does not apply at receipt** — the receipt is business income under section 28, at slab rates.

115BBH applies later, when she converts. **The two-stage structure is not practitioner commentary. It is on the face of the section.**

## ✅ Sub-section (3) closes an escape route
*"whether capital asset or not"* — so she cannot argue her way out by holding it as stock-in-trade.

## ⭐ AND HERE IS WHAT IT DOES NOT SAY

**Read all three sub-sections again. There is not one word about how to determine the amount.**

It says what rate to apply (30%), what may be deducted (cost of acquisition only), what may not be set off (anything), and what "transfer" means.

**It never says how to express the income in rupees.**

## And the Act's own general definition does not reach us either

**Section 2(22B)** defines fair market value:

> *"'fair market value', in relation to **a capital asset**, means— (i) the price that the capital asset would ordinarily fetch on sale in the open market on the relevant date; and (ii) where the price referred to in sub-clause (i) is not ascertainable, such price as may be determined in accordance with **the rules made under this Act**."*

**Two problems, both ours.**

1. **It is scoped to "a capital asset."** Our question is the value of consideration *received for services* under section 28 — not the value of a capital asset.
2. **Limb (ii) points at "the rules made under this Act"** — which is Rule 11UA, which contains zero references to virtual digital assets. See `IT-RULE-57.md`.

**The Act defines fair market value by pointing at rules. The rules do not cover the asset. That is the same closed loop, arrived at from a second direction.**
