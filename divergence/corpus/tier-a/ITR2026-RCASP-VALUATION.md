# Income-tax Rules, 2026 — the crypto valuation waterfall
<!-- VERBATIM-START -->

**Scope, from the rule's own opening words:** *"For the purposes of sub-clauses (vi), (vii), (viii) and (ix) of sub-rule (1)(e)"* — the aggregate-reporting obligations of a **reporting crypto-asset service provider** under section 509.

*(i) **the fair market value shall be determined and reported in Indian Rupee, using the valuation method as specified in sub-clause (ii)**;*

*(ii) in performing the valuation, the reporting crypto-asset service provider shall use as a reference, the values of relevant crypto-asset and Indian Rupee trading pairs **it maintains** …;*

*(iii) where the reporting crypto-asset service provider … does not maintain an applicable reference value …:*
&nbsp;&nbsp;*(A) firstly, the **internal accounting book values** maintained by the reporting crypto-asset service provider … shall be used;*
&nbsp;&nbsp;*(B) if a book value is not available, a value provided by **third-party companies or websites that aggregate current prices** … shall be used, if the valuation method used by that third party is reasonably expected to provide a reliable indicator of value;*
&nbsp;&nbsp;*(C) if neither … is available, the **most recent valuation** of the relevant crypto-asset by the reporting crypto-asset service provider shall be used; and*
&nbsp;&nbsp;***(D) if a value can still not be attributed, a reasonable estimate may be applied as a measure of last resort;***

*(iv) with respect to each relevant crypto-asset for which the reporting crypto-asset service provider has relied on an alternative valuation method outlined in sub-clause (iii), **the method shall be indicated in Form No. 167**.*

# ⭐⭐⭐ THE MOST IMPORTANT PROVISION WE HAVE FOUND

## 1. India HAS prescribed a crypto valuation method — and not for the taxpayer

This applies to a **reporting crypto-asset service provider** — an exchange, broker or wallet operator — reporting under section 509.

**It does not apply to a person computing her own taxable income.**

> **The government sat down, worked out a four-step method for valuing a crypto-asset in rupees, wrote it into the Rules — and applied it only to exchanges. The person who actually has to file a return got nothing.**

**Far stronger than an absence, and now gazette-sourced.**

## 2. ⭐⭐⭐ THE PRESCRIBED METHOD ENDS IN AN ADMISSION

| Step | Method |
|---|---|
| (ii) | The trading pairs **it maintains** |
| (iii)(A) | Its **internal accounting book values** |
| (iii)(B) | **Third-party aggregator websites** |
| (iii)(C) | Its **most recent valuation** |
| **(iii)(D)** | ***"a reasonable estimate may be applied as a measure of last resort"*** |

> ## **The only crypto valuation method India has ever prescribed ends by saying: make a reasonable estimate.**

When the State finally sat down to say how a crypto-asset should be valued in rupees, it wrote four steps and then conceded a determinate answer may not exist.

**We are not asserting this question has no settled answer. The Rules say so, in the last line of the only method they contain.**

## 3. ⭐⭐⭐ SUB-CLAUSE (iv) IS OUR PRODUCT, ALREADY MANDATED

> *"the method shall be indicated in Form No. 167"*

**Where an exchange falls back to an alternative method, it must disclose which method it used.**

**Method disclosure, required by Indian law, for crypto valuation, in force from 1 April 2026.**

| | Exchanges (RCASPs) | Our freelancer |
|---|---|---|
| Prescribed valuation waterfall | ✅ Rules 2026 | ❌ none |
| Fallback ending in "reasonable estimate" | ✅ | ❌ |
| **Duty to disclose which method was used** | ✅ **Form 167** | ❌ **nothing** |

> **India has already legislated method disclosure for crypto valuation. It applies to the exchange handling the transaction. It does not apply to the person whose income it is.**

**Our product is not a novel idea. It is the discipline India already imposes on exchanges, applied to the person who actually has to file.**

**This is the FIN 48 finding again — an established discipline that never reached the individual — except this time it is Indian law, about crypto, in force this year.**

## 4. Handling the obvious counter

*"So a method exists — why not just use the RCASP waterfall?"*

1. **Scoped by its own terms** to RCASP reporting under s.509. Not a rule for computing taxable income.
2. **She cannot follow it.** Step (ii) needs *"trading pairs it maintains."* (iii)(A) needs *"internal accounting book values."* (iii)(C) needs *"the most recent valuation by the RCASP."* **She is not an exchange. Three of four steps are unavailable to her by definition.**
3. **The one step she could use is (iii)(B)** — third-party aggregator websites — **which is exactly the CoinGecko screenshot everyone already takes.** The Rules would bless the practice, if only they applied to her.

**Say all three. The third is the one that lands.**
