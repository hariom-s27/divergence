# Rule 207 — Rate of exchange for the purpose of deduction of tax at source on income payable in foreign currency

## The rule that has the fallback. And the only place in the notified Rules that names the State Bank of India.

<!-- VERBATIM-START -->

**207. Rate of exchange for purpose of deduction of tax at source on income payable in foreign currency.—**

**(1)** *For the purpose of deduction of tax at source on any income payable in foreign currency as specified in sub-rule (2), the rate of exchange for calculation of the value in rupees shall be the telegraphic transfer buying rate of such currency as on the date on which such tax is required to be deducted under the provisions of Chapter XIX-B of the Act; **but where the telegraphic transfer buying rate is not published on such date, the last such published rate may be taken.***

**(2)** *The provisions of sub-rule (1) shall be applicable in respect of income payable—*
*(a) to an assessee outside India; or*
*(b) to a Unit located in an International Financial Services Centre; or*
*(c) by a Unit located in an International Financial Services Centre, to an assessee in India.*

**(3)** *For the purposes of this rule,—*

*(a) "International Financial Services Centre" shall have the same meaning as assigned to it in section 2(q) of the Special Economic Zones Act, 2005 (28 of 2005);*

*(b) **"telegraphic transfer buying rate", in relation to a foreign currency, means the rate or rates of exchange adopted by the State Bank of India constituted under the State Bank of India Act, 1955 (23 of 1955), for buying such currency, having regard to the guidelines specified from time to time by the Reserve Bank of India for buying such currency, where such currency is made available to that bank through a telegraphic transfer**; and*

*(c) "Unit" shall have the meaning assigned to it in section 2(zc) of the Special Economic Zones Act, 2005 (28 of 2005).*

# ⭐⭐ WHY THIS FILE EXISTS

## 1. It is the only place in the notified Rules that names the State Bank of India

Machine-checked across the full notified Income-tax Rules, 2026: **sub-rule (3)(b) is the sole provision naming the State Bank of India.**

Without it, "the SBI telegraphic transfer buying rate" in any DIVERGENCE output would have **no citation behind it at all** — it would be a market convention presented as a legal source, which is precisely the failure the project exists to catch.

**Method B is now citable.** And note carefully what it is citable *for*: the definition of the rate, in a rule that governs **payments out to non-residents**, not receipts by residents.

## 2. It contains the fallback that Rule 206 does not

Sub-rule (1), second half:

> *"…but where the telegraphic transfer buying rate is **not published on such date**, the **last such published rate** may be taken."*

**This is exactly our fact pattern.** SBI published on Thursday 25 June 2026, **skipped Friday the 26th**, and did not publish again until Monday the 29th. A payment settling at 03:14 IST on Sunday 28 June has no published rate in existence.

Rule 207 says what to do about that. **Rule 206 does not.**

## 3. And Rule 206 cites this rule — for the definition only

**Rule 206(2)(a):** *"'telegraphic transfer buying rate' shall have the meaning assigned to it in **rule 207**."*

|  | Paying **out** — r.207 | **Receiving** as a resident — r.206 |
|---|---|---|
| Defines the term | ✅ r.207(3)(b) | borrows it from r.207 by express cross-reference |
| Names SBI | ✅ | only through the borrowed definition |
| **Rate not published on the date?** | ✅ **"the last such published rate may be taken"** | ❌ **nothing** |

> ## **The fallback and the definition are in the same rule. Rule 206 imports one and not the other.**
>
> **The drafter anticipated this exact problem and wrote the fix into the rule that does not govern our case.**

## 4. Sub-rule (2) confirms the scope, and confirms it cannot be borrowed

Rule 207 applies to income payable **(a) to an assessee outside India**, or to/from an IFSC Unit. Our freelancer is a resident **receiving** from abroad. She is in none of the three limbs.

**So the fallback is not merely in a different rule — it is in a rule whose own sub-rule (2) excludes her.** You cannot argue your way into it.

# WHAT TO SAY

> *"There are two exchange-rate rules sitting next to each other. One governs money going out. One governs money coming in. The one going out says: if the rate wasn't published that day, use the last one that was. The one coming in doesn't say anything — and it borrows its definition from the rule that does. The drafter had that sentence open in front of him."*

**Two instances of the same asymmetry — this, and Rule 57's column B.** One looks like an oversight. Two looks like the shape of the instrument.
