# Rule 247 — Qualification of registered valuer for the purposes of section 514

## ⭐⭐⭐ The Rules create a registered valuer of virtual digital assets — and give that valuer nothing to work with.

<!-- VERBATIM-START -->

**247. Qualification of registered valuer for the purposes of section 514.—**

**(1)** *For the purposes of section 514(2), the qualifications for registration as valuers, for different classes of assets shall be as specified in sub-rules (2) to (9).*

**(2)** *The qualifications for registration as valuers, for classes of assets mentioned in column B of the following Table shall be as specified in column C of the said Table:* [Table follows — immovable property, agricultural land, plantations, forest, mines and quarries, stocks and shares, machinery and plant, jewellery, works of art, life interest, actuarial. **No entry for virtual digital assets.**]

**(3)** *The qualifications for a valuer for assets, other than the assets covered in sub-rule (2), shall be determined by the Principal Chief Commissioner or Chief Commissioner, or the Principal Director General or Director General, to whom application for getting registered as a valuer has been submitted and **the decision of Principal Chief Commissioner or Chief Commissioner, or the Principal Director General or Director General in this regard shall be conclusive.***

**(4)** *No person shall qualify for registration as a valuer, other than as a valuer of works of art **or virtual digital assets** or other class of assets as may be specified by the Board in this behalf, if he is employed under Government or any other employer.*

# ⭐⭐⭐ THE FIFTH LOCKED DOOR — AND THE ONLY ONE THAT IS A PRESENCE RATHER THAN AN ABSENCE

**Sub-rule (4) names, in a statutory instrument notified on 20 March 2026, "a valuer of works of art or virtual digital assets."**

The category exists. It is written down. It is written down **twice** — once in the rule and once in the form. A person can apply.

Now follow what that person is actually given:

| What a valuer of virtual digital assets would need | What the Rules provide |
|---|---|
| **A class of asset to register for** | ❌ Form 169's list has **eleven** classes. Works of art is number 9. **Virtual digital assets is not on the list.** The applicant must select "Any other asset" |
| **Prescribed qualifications** | ❌ **Sub-rule (3)** — for assets outside sub-rule (2), the qualification *"shall be determined by the Principal Chief Commissioner… and the decision… **shall be conclusive**."* Officer discretion. No criteria. No appeal |
| **A prescribed method to apply** | ❌ **Rule 57 — zero references to virtual digital assets**, machine-checked against this gazette |

> ## **The Rules name a professional valuer of virtual digital assets in sub-rule (4), decline to give that valuer a class of asset in Form 169, decline to state their qualifications in sub-rule (3), and decline to give them a method in Rule 57.**

## Why this is the sharpest of the five doors

The other four are absences:

| # | Door | Why it is locked |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Rule 57** — fair market value | Scoped to jewellery, art, shares, immovable property; the residual clause is scoped to s.26(2)(j), not s.92 |
| 2 | **s.2(22B)** — fair market value generally | Scoped to *"a capital asset"*; points at rules that do not reach a VDA |
| 3 | **s.115BBH** — 30% on VDA transfer | Contains the word "value" **zero** times |
| 4 | **Rule 206** — rate of exchange | Converts *foreign currency*; a VDA is defined as not being foreign currency |
| **5** | **Rule 247(4)** — the registered valuer | ⭐ **Not an absence. A presence with nothing behind it** |

An absence is always arguable — *"you didn't look hard enough."* A **presence** is not. The drafter wrote the words *"valuer of virtual digital assets"* into the Rules. He knew the category existed. He was thinking about it in the same instrument in which he declined to say how to value one.

> *"Four of the doors are locked. This one has a nameplate on it and no room behind it."*

## And it closes the last escape route

**"So get a registered valuer's report."** That is the natural professional answer, and it is what Rule 57 itself offers for jewellery (row 1(c)) and artistic work (row 2(c)) where value exceeds ₹50,000.

**She cannot.** There is no class of asset to register under, no stated qualification for the registrant, and no method for the registrant to apply. The Rules contemplate the profession and provide none of its content.
