# Rule 56 — Meaning of expressions used in determination of fair market value

## The rule that fixes the valuation **date**, immediately before the rule that declines to fix the **method**.

<!-- VERBATIM-START -->

**56. Meaning of expressions used in determination of fair market value.–** *For the purposes of this rule and rule 57, —*

*…*

*(f) "registered valuer" shall have the same meaning as assigned to it in section 513;*

*…*

*(i) **"valuation date" means the date provided in the Table below:***

**Table**

| Sl. No — A | **Section — B** | **Valuation date — C** |
|---|---|---|
| **1.** | **Section 92** | ***Date on which property or consideration, as the case may be, referred to in section 92 is received by the assessee.*** |
| 2. | *Section 79* | *Date on which the capital asset, being share of a company other than a quoted share, referred to in section 79, is transferred.* |
| 3. | *Section 26(2)(j)* | *Date on which the inventory is converted, or treated, as a capital asset.* |

<!-- VERBATIM-END -->

# ⭐⭐ WHY THIS FILE IS LOAD-BEARING

## The Rules prescribe the day. Four lines later they decline to prescribe the method.

Rule 56 opens *"For the purposes of this rule **and rule 57**"* — the two rules are a single mechanism, and the drafter says so.

**Rule 56(i) row 1** fixes the valuation date for a section 92 receipt with complete precision: *"the date on which property or consideration … is received by the assessee."* Not the last day of the month. Not the last day of the tax year. **The date of receipt.**

**Rule 57's table** — the very next rule, on the same page — then lists the property types for which a method is provided, and a virtual digital asset is not among them, and the one residual clause that would have caught it is scoped away from section 92.

> ## **The gap is not vagueness about timing. The timing is prescribed to the day. It is the method that is absent.**
>
> ## **The Rules ask a question of themselves — "what was this worth on the day it arrived?" — and, four lines later, decline to answer it.**

## Why this is the best available *general* framing

The project has needed a widening that is general enough for a non-Indian judge and specific enough to be checkable. Everything tried so far — *"every regulated field has gaps"* — is true, general, and unevidenced.

**This is a widening that costs twenty seconds and is proved from one page of one document:**

> *"This rulebook tells you the exact day to value the payment. Four lines later, it declines to tell you how. That is not vagueness — the timing is prescribed to the day. It is a rule that asks a question of itself and doesn't answer."*

A judge can check it. No legal training required, no crypto, no exchange data, no rupee arithmetic. **It is strictly stronger than an assertion about regulated domains in general, and it is free.**

## And it makes the Sunday fact legally load-bearing rather than merely vivid

Because the valuation date is **the date of receipt**, the fact that the receipt landed at 03:14 IST on Sunday 28 June 2026 is not colour. It is the operative date the Rules themselves nominate — and it is a date on which:

- **SBI published nothing** (published 25 June, skipped the 26th, next on the 29th — a four-day hole, archived with a PDF link per row: `SBI-TTBR-DATA.md`)
- **FBIL published nothing** (weekdays only, excluding Saturday, Sunday and Mumbai bank holidays: `FBIL-METHODOLOGY.md`)
- **The market had a continuous price** (CoinDCX daily candle: open 102.83 · high 103.41 · low 102.31 · close 102.83)

> **The Rules nominate a date. On that date, every official source is silent and the market is not.**
